Why We Do This

Here's a funny thing about money: most of it is invisible. The dollars in your bank are entries in a database. The reserves backing your stablecoins are described in PDFs you'll never read, audited by firms you've never heard of, stored in jurisdictions chosen for their opacity.

We thought it would be interesting to try the opposite. What if you could literally see the money? What if "proof of reserves" meant pointing a camera at a pile of coins and saying "look, there they are"?

It's a little absurd. We know. That's part of the point.

A Note on Fort Knox

For context.

The U.S. Mint reports that Fort Knox holds 147.4 million fine troy ounces of gold. Treasury controls and inspections have occurred over time, including a 1970s review observed by the U.S. Government Accountability Office.

That is not the same thing as a public live view or an open, continuously observable count.

We are not asserting that gold is missing. We are saying that public visibility is a design choice. CENT publishes its claim, live token supply, and limitations together—and offers vault visits by appointment. Read the GAO review →
